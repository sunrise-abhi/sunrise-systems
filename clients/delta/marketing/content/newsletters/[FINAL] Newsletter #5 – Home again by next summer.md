**Subject: Home again by next summer**

It’s been almost three months since the LA wildfires, and many in our community are facing the unimaginable task of rebuilding after losing everything and there still seems to be a lot of confusion and frustration. 

The emotional toll of your house burning down is crushing enough—before you even consider the practical nightmare of where to live, what to do with kids and pets, and how to navigate insurance.  
And on top of all that, people are hearing horror stories about rebuilding potentially taking *3-5 years*, making them question if they can even go through with the process.

The good news is that from what we’ve seen, these timeline fears are mostly unfounded. With the right team, I think a lot of fire-affected homeowners can be back in their homes in an 18-24  month timeline.

The mayor has waived planning review for rebuilds within 10% of the original footprint, which sounds great on paper. In my opinion this has been misleading to a lot of people we have talked with. Plans still need to be drawn, submitted and approved and that takes time. And there’s a major gray area they haven't addressed, which is to build back better. Structurally sound so you’re prepared for the next disaster, environmentally friendly because it’s the right thing to do and because it doesn’t have to cost more, and of course fire resistant. 

When they say they're "waiving planning review," that doesn't mean you don't need permits at all. You still need permits, and your plans still need to be submitted and approved.

What's unclear right now is exactly which current building codes will be strictly enforced for fire rebuilds. Will rebuilt homes need to meet current seismic standards? (Yes) Will they need to comply with Title 24 Part 6 energy efficiency requirements? (Yes) We're actively working to get clarity from Building and Safety on these questions so we can give homeowners accurate information about what's required.

One thing’s for sure: licensed architects and engineers still have both legal and ethical obligations not to submit plans that don't meet code. There will definitely be some groups cutting corners, but reputable professionals won't risk their licenses or your safety.

And timeline concerns aside, the responsible approach is always to design homes that are fire-resistant, seismically sound, and energy efficient—regardless of any temporary waivers. These standards exist for good reasons.

With all that said, from what we’ve been seeing, a realistic timeline for most residential rebuilds looks like this: 2-6 months for plan development, 3-12 months for permits, and 9-12 months for construction. Meaning homeowners who start now could find themselves back in their newly rebuilt homes as early as next summer.

The biggest challenge isn't going to be the permits or construction timeline—it'll be finding available licensed professionals to take on the work. With over 13,000 structures affected by the fires, the harsh reality is that there are only so many quality builders across LA, and each one can only handle a limited number of rebuild projects while maintaining their standards.
